Sample routing

A typical 7 days, day by day

This is the Masai Mara Signature routing — open any day to see what it actually feels like. Yours will be different; that's the point.

Land at JKIA and your driver is waiting in arrivals with a name board — no queue for a taxi, no haggling. 45 minutes to your hotel in Nairobi's Karen district, where the traffic thins and the trees take over.

Tonight: dinner on the terrace, and a 20-minute call with your guide to plan tomorrow's departure time around you.

The road route crosses the Rift escarpment for your first full view of the valley — five hours with two coffee stops. Flying takes 45 minutes and we'll recommend which suits your jet lag.

After lunch at camp, out for the 4pm drive: the light softens, lion moves, and the plains empty of day-trippers.

Optional: a 45-minute balloon ride at 6am, floating low over the herds, landing to a champagne bush breakfast. On the ground, your guide drives the same territory on wheels — two perspectives, one morning.

Sample day rhythm: 6:15am departure · 9:30 breakfast in the field · midday rest at camp · 3:30pm out again until the light goes.

Morning at the Talek and Mara river crossings — from July, this is where wildebeest hesitate, and where crocodile and leopard wait.

Late afternoon, a visit to a Maasai manyatta arranged directly with the families we've worked with for a decade: entry fees go to them, not an intermediary.

South through Narok town for lunch, arriving at Nakuru in the afternoon. The park is compact, so distances are short and sightings frequent — white rhino, waterbuck, and Rothschild's giraffe browsing the acacia.

A late start and a relaxed drive back, arriving mid-afternoon. Time for a shower, the Giraffe Centre or the Karen Blixen Museum, then dinner with your guide to unpack the week's sightings.

Timed to your flight, with a stop for last-minute coffee and crafts. Or skip the airport: a 1-hour flight to Diani adds four nights of Indian Ocean to the same itinerary.

Everything travellers ask us before booking

July to October brings the Great Migration in the Masai Mara and dry, easy wildlife viewing. January to March is hottest and driest with fewer vehicles, while the long rains in April–May mean green landscapes and our lowest rates. There is wildlife in every month — the right time depends on what you want to see, and we'll tell you straight if your dates aren't ideal.

Yes. We operate in the national parks and reserves where rangers patrol daily, and we move between towns in daylight with our own guides. Our team monitors travel advisories daily and reroutes when needed, and you have our WhatsApp number 24/7 for the whole trip. Nairobi itself deserves ordinary big-city caution — we arrange your transfers so you're never waiting on a street corner.

Park entrance fees, a private 4x4 Land Cruiser with a roof hatch, fuel, an English-speaking Kenyan guide, airport transfers, full-board accommodation and bottled water. Not included: international flights, visas, tips and personal purchases. Your quotation lists every line — if it isn't written down, you aren't paying for it.

A 30% deposit confirms your dates, with the balance due 30 days before arrival. We accept card, bank transfer and mobile money (M-Pesa), and you receive a proper invoice and signed contract. Nothing is charged until you have approved your itinerary in writing.

Most nationalities need Kenya's eTA (electronic travel authorisation), applied for online before departure — we send you the exact link and a walkthrough. Yellow fever vaccination is only required if arriving from an endemic country. Anti-malaria tablets are a personal choice; we'll give you the local guidance and a pre-departure checklist once your trip is confirmed.

Cancel more than 60 days before arrival and your deposit is refunded less a $100 admin fee. Between 60 and 30 days, 50% of the trip cost is retained by the lodges. Inside 30 days we retain the full amount, but we'll transfer your booking to any date within 12 months at no charge — a flexible date has saved more holidays than a refund ever could.

Tipping is never expected and never requested. If your guide earns it, $10–20 per person per day is the common range, paid in cash in shillings or dollars. Your guide will never ask you for a tip.

Yes — Kenya is brilliant with children. There is no minimum age on a private safari, many lodges reduce rates for under-12s, and we shorten game drives, add junior-ranger activities and choose family tents or connecting rooms. Tell us their ages and we'll build the days around their attention span, not ours.
